To guide someone, “that way” is not enough. Use precise words and put instructions in the correct order.

## Position and movement

Describe positions with left, right, in front, behind, above, below, between, near and far. A map represents a space from above; landmarks help locate an object.

To code a movement on a grid, chain instructions: “move forward 3 squares”, “turn a quarter-turn right”, “move forward 2 squares”. In CE1, a route can contain up to fifteen instructions.

## Recognise symmetry

A line of symmetry splits a shape into two parts that match when folded. In CE2, pupils check lines by folding or tracing paper, then complete a shape on a grid.

<Callout title="Mind the viewpoint">
The right side of a person facing you is your left. When coding a route, always keep the viewpoint of the moving character.
</Callout>

## Exercises

1. A robot faces north, turns right and moves forward. Which direction does it travel?
2. How many lines of symmetry does a non-square rectangle have?
3. Does a capital A have a vertical line of symmetry?

<QuickCheck answer="1. East · 2. Two · 3. Yes" />
