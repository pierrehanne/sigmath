A cereal box, a ball and an ice-cream cone do not have the same shape. Objects occupy space; their geometric shapes are **solids**.

## Recognise and describe

Cycle 2 pupils recognise cubes, cuboids, spheres, cylinders, cones and pyramids. Polyhedra are described using:

- **faces**, the flat surfaces around them;
- **edges**, where two faces meet;
- **vertices**, where several edges meet.

A cube has 6 square faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. A net is a flat arrangement of faces that folds into a solid.

<Callout title="See what is hidden">
In a perspective drawing, some faces and edges cannot be seen. They still exist; hidden edges may appear as dotted lines.
</Callout>

## Exercises

1. Which geometric solid resembles a drinks can?
2. How many faces does a cuboid have, and what shapes are they?
3. How many faces does a square-based pyramid have altogether?

<QuickCheck answer="1. A cylinder · 2. 6 rectangular faces (some may be square) · 3. 5 faces" />
