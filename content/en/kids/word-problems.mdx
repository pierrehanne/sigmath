A problem is a short story with missing information. Solving it means understanding the situation before choosing a calculation.

## Four steps

1. **Understand**: what do we know and what are we looking for?
2. **Model**: draw bars, groups or a line.
3. **Calculate**: write and perform the operation.
4. **Answer**: check, then write a sentence with the unit.

Learn to recognise different families: find the whole or a part, compare quantities, repeat equal groups, share or group. Problems grow from one to several steps across the cycle.

## Guided example

A library owns 46 picture books. It receives 18 and lends 9:

$$46 + 18 = 64 \qquad 64 - 9 = 55$$

The library now has **55 picture books**.

<Callout title="A useful habit">
Words such as “more” or “less” do not choose the operation for you. Represent what happens to the quantities.
</Callout>

## Exercises

1. Zoe has 17 cards, 5 more than Sam. How many does Sam have?
2. Share 24 pens equally between 4 pots.
3. A bus carries 28 people. 7 leave and 12 enter. How many are on the bus now?

<QuickCheck answer="1. 12 cards · 2. 6 pens · 3. 33 people" />
