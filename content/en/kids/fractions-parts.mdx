A strip of paper represents one unit. Fold it into four exactly equal parts: each part is one quarter of the unit.

## Parts must be equal

In $\frac{3}{8}$, the bottom number says that the unit has 8 equal parts; the top number says that we take 3.

<FractionSlicer />

In CE1, fractions are parts of a whole: pupils name, represent and compare them, and add fractions with the same denominator. In CE2, a fraction is also a **number** on a number line and can be greater than 1.

$$\frac{7}{4} = 1 + \frac{3}{4}$$

<Callout title="Essential condition">
The parts must be equal. Three pieces of different sizes do not make three thirds.
</Callout>

## Exercises

1. Imagine 3 parts coloured on a disc split into 5 equal parts. What fraction is coloured?
2. Compare $\frac{2}{6}$ and $\frac{5}{6}$.
3. Calculate $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{8}$.
