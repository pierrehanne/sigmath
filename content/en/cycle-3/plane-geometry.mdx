In geometry, properties identify a shape—not its orientation or appearance.

## Relations and constructions

Primary school consolidates parallelism, perpendicularity, symmetry and properties of triangles and quadrilaterals. Pupils reproduce figures with ruler, set square and compasses, then write construction programs.

Year 7 introduces perpendicular bisectors, angle bisectors, chords and circumcircles. A segment’s perpendicular bisector contains every point equally distant from its endpoints.

<Callout title="A short proof">
If M belongs to the perpendicular bisector of [AB], then $MA = MB$. This property justifies rather than merely observes.
</Callout>

To construct a triangle from three side lengths, draw two circular arcs. They only intersect when the lengths can actually form a triangle.

## Exercises

1. Give two properties of a rhombus.
2. Where are the points equally distant from A and B?
3. Can lengths 2 cm, 3 cm and 7 cm form a triangle?

<QuickCheck answer="1. Four equal sides and parallel opposite sides · 2. On the perpendicular bisector of [AB] · 3. No" />
