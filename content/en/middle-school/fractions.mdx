A pizza lands in the middle of the table. It is round and whole — then someone slices it. That is where fractions begin: **one quantity seen as several equal parts**.

## One part of a whole

If the pizza is cut into four equal slices and you take one, you have taken $\frac{1}{4}$ of the pizza. The bottom number, the **denominator**, tells us how many equal parts make up the whole. The top number, the **numerator**, tells us how many parts we mean.

## Same amount, different notation

Two quarters cover exactly the same amount as one half:

$$\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

We call these **equivalent fractions**. Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number does not change the amount represented.

<Callout title="Remember">
A fraction is not two separate numbers. It describes a relationship: how many parts, out of how many equal parts.
</Callout>
